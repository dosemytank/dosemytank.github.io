Free aquarium tools for planted tank enthusiasts

Your aquarium. Your formula.

Prepare your own fertilizers. Calculate nutrient concentrations. Remineralize RO water. DoseMyTank tells you how many grams of each salt go into the bottle, how many ml to dose and what that adds to your tank in mg/L (ppm).

What you can calculate

Four tools cover what most planted tank keepers need. Each one shows its working: the salts, the grams, the dose and the resulting concentration.

DIY fertilizer from dry salts

Enter your tank volume, light and CO₂ and choose a plan – the app picks the salts and the dose. You get grams per bottle, ml per dose and the expected weekly increase of NO₃, PO₄, K, Mg and Fe.

  • EI (Estimative Index), PPS-Pro and PMDD recipes, or your own
  • Macro and micro bottles, or dry dosing
  • Warnings when salts would precipitate (e.g. calcium with phosphate) or not dissolve
  • A shopping list with common names (potassium nitrate, Epsom salt…)

RO water remineralization

How much gypsum, magnesium sulfate and potassium bicarbonate to add to a batch of RO water for your target GH and KH, with the Ca : Mg ratio you choose.

  • Calcium and magnesium already in your fertilizer are taken into account
  • Step-by-step preparation, with a check of GH and KH
  • Conductivity (µS/cm) and TDS of the prepared water

Commercial fertilizer dosing

Over 30 fertilizers in the library (Tropica, Seachem, Easy Life, CSM+B and more). Scale the manufacturer's dose to your tank, see what your dose adds in mg/L, or get the ml needed for a target.

  • Compare up to three fertilizers side by side
  • Add your own product from its label (% or g/L, K₂O and P₂O₅ converted)
  • Entries without a full published composition are marked as approximate

Tank log

Water tests, water changes, doses and notes. Charts with your target range, a reminder schedule you can export to your phone's calendar, and a hint on what to check first.

  • Several tanks
  • Backup to a file and export to CSV
  • Nutrient concentration of a bottle
  • DIY clone of a commercial fertilizer
  • Correct a test result to target
  • Water change calculator
  • CO₂ from pH and KH
  • Unit converters: N ↔ NO₃, P₂O₅ ↔ PO₄, K₂O ↔ K, °dH ↔ ppm
  • Tank volume from dimensions
  • PRO: titration, KH lowering with acid, salt build-up

Example: your first bottle of fertilizer

  1. Describe your tank100 L (26 US gal) of water, low light, no CO₂, 30% water change a week.
  2. Choose a plan“Recommended” – a typical starting dose for your light. Lean, More and EI-style plans are one tap away.
  3. Tick the salts you haveThe default set is the four basic salts plus a CSM+B trace mix. Salts that can't share a bottle are split automatically.
  4. Mix and doseWeigh the salts into two 500 ml bottles and dose 30 ml from each, three times a week. A bottle lasts about 6 weeks.
  5. Test and adjustTest NO₃ and PO₄ before water changes for the first 2–3 weeks, log the results and move the plan up or down.

For beginners and for experienced dosers

If you're new to dry fertilizers

  • Start with “Make fertilizer”: answer a few questions and get a complete recipe.
  • Plain-language explanations of GH, KH, ppm, chelates and macro vs micro, plus help screens.
  • Clear warnings: copper with shrimp, ammonium, salts that won't dissolve, acids.
  • Unsure about your plants? The plant problems and algae checklists suggest what to test first.

If you already know EI and PPS-Pro

  • Macro and Micro calculators, salt by salt, with your own targets per dose or per week.
  • Every hydrate form (e.g. MgSO₄ vs MgSO₄·7H₂O), salt purity and chelate % from the label.
  • Solubility checks, common-ion effects and automatic bottle splitting.
  • Your own salts, recipes and fertilizer library – exportable as a file.

Install and your data

DoseMyTank is a web app. You can install it on your phone or computer straight from the browser – no app store needed.

iPhone and iPad (Safari)

  1. Open this page in Safari (not inside the Facebook app).
  2. Tap Share (the square with an arrow).
  3. Choose Add to Home Screen.

Android (Chrome)

  1. Open this page in Chrome.
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu in the top right corner.
  3. Choose Install app or “Add to Home screen”.

Computer (Chrome, Edge)

  1. Open the app.
  2. Click the install icon at the right end of the address bar.
  • No account, no login. Nothing is sent to a server.
  • Your data is stored in your browser. After the first visit the app works offline, and it updates itself when you're online.
  • Save a backup to a file and load it on another device in Settings.
Your data belongs to this browser. Always open the app from the same home screen icon and make a backup now and then. A new phone or clearing browser data removes it.
Acids and chemicals. Whenever a calculation involves acid, the app shows safety rules and a milder option (e.g. 10% vinegar). Add chemicals diluted and one at a time.

Frequently asked questions

Is DoseMyTank really free?

Yes. There's no paid version, no ads and no account. The app is a hobby project and runs entirely in your browser. If you'd like to support further development, you can buy me a coffee on Ko-fi.

Where do I start?

Open the app – the first screen is “Make fertilizer”. Enter your tank volume and choose light and CO₂. The recipe and the shopping list appear straight away for a basic set of salts.

Which dosing methods does it support?

EI (Estimative Index), PPS-Pro and PMDD as ready recipes, plus your own targets in mg/L per week or per dose. You can dose from bottles or dry dose straight into the tank. Commercial fertilizers can be combined with your own salts.

My Epsom salt is MgSO₄·7H₂O, but a recipe says MgSO₄. What now?

Each form of a salt is a separate choice, e.g. MgSO₄·7H₂O (heptahydrate, Epsom salt) and anhydrous MgSO₄. Pick the one on your package. The amount of magnesium stays the same – only the grams you weigh out change.

Can I use gallons?

The app calculates in liters and milliliters, as most dosing recipes do. When you enter your tank volume, tap “Tank in gallons? Convert to liters” to convert US gallons.

Is it ppm or mg/L?

For fresh water they're the same: 1 mg/L = 1 ppm. The app shows mg/L; fertilizer concentrates are shown in g/L.

What's the difference between remineralization and correcting parameters?

RO remineralization calculates how much salt to add to a whole batch of RO water before a water change, so it reaches the target GH and KH. Correcting tank parameters calculates how much of a nutrient to add to the water already in the tank to move a test result to your target.

How accurate are the results?

The calculation is exact for the data you enter: molar masses come from the chemical formulas (IUPAC 2021 atomic weights), and the math is covered by automated tests. What you get in the tank also depends on salt purity, your scale, the label composition and drop test accuracy. Solubility of salt mixes is estimated with a safety margin. Some commercial fertilizers don't publish a full composition – those are marked as approximate. The app doesn't predict how much plants will take up – only a water test shows that.

Will it tell me whether my plants will grow well?

No. It calculates what you add to the water. Plant growth also depends on light, CO₂, substrate and species. The recommended doses are a starting point you adjust after testing and watching your plants.

How do I move my data to a new phone?

In Settings, save a backup to a file, then load it on the new phone in the same place. Without a backup the data stays in the old browser.

I found a bug or have an idea.

Please report it on the feedback page. For bugs, include your phone model, browser and what exactly happened – a screenshot helps.
